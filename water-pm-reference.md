## The PM work

The recurring shape, from your own Slack and Asana and from watching PM work across the pods:

- **Grant lifecycle** — a grant moves from investigation through Conditional Approval (the internal recommendation document) to a published grant page, then into payments, reporting, and renewal. The PM keeps the timeline, the paperwork, and the people moving through that path, and posts grant announcements in #grant-updates.
- **Grant pages and vets** — getting a grant page ready, filing the Commons vet request, reviewing the vet, and arranging the grantee's review of the page.
- **Grants administration** — conditional payments (releasing funds once stated conditions are met), LOI requests, due diligence, financial reporting reminders for non-501(c)(3) grantees, final reports, and returns of unspent funds. Grants Administration (Laura Portko's team) generates many of these as Asana tasks addressed to you; #cross-team-grant-logistics is where the cross-team threads live.
- **Running the pod's rhythm** — the weekly pod meeting agenda, action items afterwards, and the running docs for recurring external syncs (the World Bank sync among them).
- **Events, travel, and site visits** — briefing notes, calendars, and speaker notes for conferences (World Water Week 2026; a UNC conference in late October), and site-visit logistics. Live now: the [Madagascar site visit, October 8–16, 2026](https://docs.google.com/document/d/12U5d-krg--gRAEVBoc8Qcrp0dXJWAVxTaHb8CdZtejU/edit) with Megan and Andrew, to see UNICEF's and Watalux's in-line chlorination sites; the planning doc has a tab per partner and a learning agenda.
- **Grantee and partner coordination** — check-ins, newsletters from the water funders group, and fielding grantee questions (on the chlorination CEA, for instance).
- **Keeping the pod's tracking honest** — Asana, trackers, and status updates. A wrong date, amount, or payment condition propagates to everyone who reads it, which is why the don't-invent rule in `CLAUDE.md` binds hardest here.

Partners and grantees that show up around your work: Evidence Action (DSW and ILC), Aquaya, Mangrove, Watalux, UNICEF Madagascar, Hackney House Group, the World Bank, Dimagi, and PSI.

## Where AI helps and where it fails at PM work

Calibrated to what GiveWell PMs have actually found, not generic advice:

**Where it's strong:**
- **Taking rules and applying them to the next version.** Once you articulate a rule ("anything touching the Reservations section of a CA gets extra caution"), AI applies it consistently at volume. Template conformance, checklist enforcement, and status-roundup formats all fit this shape.
- **Meeting processing.** Transcript to structured notes, decisions, and owned action items — `/process-meeting` exists because this works. The PMs who use AI heavily (Zach McLeod on Nutrition, Hunter Davis-Darby, Sam Aman on Malaria) started here.
- **Aggregating across sources.** Pulling a picture together from calendars, Drive, Asana, Granola, and Slack — the thing you already use Claude for most. A grant's status lives across a CA, a Grants Administration task, a Slack thread, and a grantee call; Claude is good at assembling those into one view with links.
- **Tracker upkeep and triage.** Reading Asana, flagging what's overdue or slipping, drafting the weekly roundup. It surfaces; you decide.
- **Drafting to a standard.** First drafts of grantee emails, LOI and reporting requests, pod-meeting action items, site-visit briefs, and grant-announcement posts against GiveWell's writing standards, checked with `/legibility-review`.

**Where it fails, in ways that matter for this seat:**
- **Silent omission.** A Docs reader that walks paragraphs only will drop every table — on a GiveWell CA that's roughly a quarter of the document, including the budget and cost-effectiveness tables. Clean-looking output is exactly what survives review, so check for the absence of things, not just the correctness of what's there.
- **It can't see comments and cell notes** in Sheets and Docs, where a lot of GiveWell's actual reasoning and status lives. A tracker read that ignores comments can be confidently stale.
- **It invents reconciliations.** Given conflicting sources it will manufacture a story that resolves them ("this document must be later, so it wins"). Pin the source of truth explicitly — for grant content, GiveWell's working rule is that **the CA is the source of truth**: even where it's imperfect, it's the researcher's own words and reasoning.
- **It misses context that lives outside the document:** the Slack thread where a date slipped, the grantee call where an owner changed, the call that happened without a calendar invite, or what you saw on a site visit. That context is your job to supply — which is what this repo's meeting notes and session logs are for.
- **Wrong-but-fluent.** Errors arrive with the same confidence as correct output. For anything feeding a shared tracker, going to a grantee, or leaving the building, a human reads it first — that's the draft-don't-send rule, not a formality.

## Growing your own skills

When a workflow repeats, write it up as a skill in `.claude/skills/` so the next run starts from the pinned-down version. Obvious first candidates for this seat: a grant-page vet request (pull the pipeline ID, links, lead investigator, and polish level from the grant's docs and fill the Commons form text), a financial-reporting or LOI reminder to a grantee, the weekly pod-meeting agenda, or a site-visit prep pack (itinerary, partner briefs, learning agenda). Small automations that replace a recurring manual task are the shape to look for.
