# GiveWell Legibility Guidance (summary)

A short version of GiveWell's writing standards, so the `legibility-review` skill (from the givewell-research-skills plugin) has a local reference to load. Full source: [LLM Writing Guidelines: GiveWell Legibility Standards](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1aqI9oXGo-YLIPvifob-bRd0WThH7mQNOyvyoTikT62o/edit).

## Core principles
- **Bottom line first.** State the conclusion in the first paragraph. Don't build to a reveal.
- **Specific over vague.** Use numbers and named sources, not "many," "some," "several."
- **Name uncertainty.** Say what's uncertain, how much it could change the conclusion, and what would resolve it. Quantify when you can ("60–70% confident").
- **Real opinions.** "My best guess is X," not "there are several considerations."
- **Falsifiable claims.** Specific enough to be wrong, not hedged into meaninglessness.
- **Intuition before numbers.** Explain why before showing the calculation.
- **Match depth to investigation.** If it was a quick look, say so. Don't dress up a surface take as authoritative.

## Style tells to cut
- No em-dashes; no "it's not X, it's Y" constructions; no false balance.
- No throat-clearing ("The reason I'm not recommending it:") or closing "open questions" sections that don't move the ask.
- Cut intensifiers ("really," "actually," "honestly"); keep honest hedges ("I think," "I'm not sure").

## For non-technical readers
Translate jargon by default: "document lookup" not "RAG," "the bot" not "hosted backend," "test set" not "eval set."
